\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\begin{center}\fontsize{16}{21}\selectfont\bfseries\thesistitle\end{center}

Local surface rework requires coordinating tool motion, contact force, and time within a selected region. This thesis connects human region specification and demonstrations to an executable local robotic polishing skill. A joint motion--force representation temporally pairs Cartesian tool pose with teaching-derived target force. Conditional Flow Matching generates action sequences from RGB, pose, measured force, and force history. A shared executor conditions the references for an existing force-feedback controller. Center-relative position learning and an explicit planar direction adapter support reuse at registered placements.

Three evaluations address strategy, force information, and spatial reuse. E1 compares rules, force-inclusive demonstration replay (T), and the force-conditioned policy (C). Each strategy completes the prescribed contact--loaded-pass--exit procedure in five observed runs. A separate additional rule-retiming diagnostic (\(R^{*}\)) is kept distinct from the original rule cohort. Mean completion times \(\pm\) sample SD are \EoneRstarTime\(\pm\)\EoneRstarTimeSD, \EoneTTime\(\pm\)\EoneTTimeSD, and \EoneCTime\(\pm\)\EoneCTimeSD~s for \(R^{*}\)/T/C. T has lower time dispersion and excess-force exposure than C. Retiming reduces the rule mean by \RstarTimeGain~s (\RstarTimePercent\%) without matching measured mean speed.

E2 separates pose-only learning with an external force target (A), motion--force learning with masked force observations (B), and motion--force learning with measured-force conditioning (C). A completes four of five attempts; B/C complete five each. Completed operator-based intervals are \EtwoATime\(\pm\)\EtwoATimeSD, \EtwoBTime\(\pm\)\EtwoBTimeSD, and \EtwoCTime\(\pm\)\EtwoCTimeSD~s, with \(n=4,5,5\). C's mean is \BCSeconds~s (\BCPercent\%) shorter than B's. These descriptive intervals use a different completion protocol from E1.

E3 reuses the same C checkpoint without new teaching or retraining. Original RGB reprocessing with unchanged saved annotations, ROI, and gray threshold reproduces the board-marker ink-area measurements. N/P1/D1/D2 contain \EthreeMainN{} selected runs and \EthreeImageN{} evaluable pairs. Removal means \(\pm\) sample SD are \EthreeNRemoval\(\pm\)\EthreeNSD, \EthreePoneRemoval\(\pm\)\EthreePoneSD, \EthreeDoneRemoval\(\pm\)\EthreeDoneSD, and \EthreeDtwoRemoval\(\pm\)\EthreeDtwoSD\%, with \(n=5,5,4,5\); final tool occlusion remains unquantified.

The results establish an executable human-guided skill, observed force-configuration differences, and reuse with registration and direction adaptation in the tested planar placements. Rules and replay remain effective fixed-task alternatives. Image-area reduction measures visible marker removal, separately from procedural completion; metal removal depth, mass, roughness, and micro-scratch restoration are not independently measured.
